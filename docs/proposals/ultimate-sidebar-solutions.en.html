Approach 1: No bar · soft-pill full-row highlight (out of the bar trap for good)

Why did we keep getting bitten by "the bar sits on top of the text" and "it escaped again"? Because the traditional "3px white bar on the left" is an old design with a structural flaw: it forces extra blank space on the left to host the bar, and any global padding washes it out.

The better modern answer: do what Linear, Raycast, Slack and native macOS do — throw the hard-edged bar away and use a soft, slightly rounded, semi-transparent full-row wash (Soft Pill Highlight).

    💡 Deep dive into the architecture decision

    🎯
    Why is "de-barring" inevitable for every modern professional tool?
    Cursor, the modern VS Code themes and Linear all removed the hard highlight bar on the left within the last two years and switched to a faintly translucent full-row background. That not only leaves zero text-alignment risk, it also stays rock-solid on mobile, touch screens and narrow viewports.
    ⚡
    Why does the accordion (approach 2) suit 20+ projects?
    With 25 projects, plain collapsing needs only three expanded groups to push the list past two thousand pixels. Auto-collapse the rest and keep just the current project plus its running background tasks, and the sidebar stays inside a single, severely restrained screen at all times.
