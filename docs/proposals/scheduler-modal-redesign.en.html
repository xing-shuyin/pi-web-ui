Scheduled-task create/edit modal redesign

Three carefully worked-out, high-fidelity redesigns for the current scheduled-task modal, which is only 460px wide, lists everything in one column and one row, crams the Prompt area, and offers neither a natural-language schedule reading nor a trigger preview.

Original pain point 1 · Cramped visuals
460px wide, stacked in one column
Reuses the base modal size and flattens all 10 fields vertically, so it reads like a spreadsheet with no room to breathe — cheap.
Original pain point 2 · Crippled Prompt
The core instruction area is only 4 rows tall
The whole point of a scheduled task is the concrete instruction (Prompt) you give the agent, yet the tiny box makes a multi-step inspection script nearly impossible to write.
Original pain point 3 · No mental feedback on the schedule
Raw Cron / bare presets
After picking or typing a Cron there is no plain-language reading and no next-run preview, so the user has no idea what will actually happen.
Fix lever · Root solution
Structural sections + wide canvas + smart schedule translation
Card and column layout, a large Prompt surface plus scenario templates, a visualized Cron preview, and advanced options tucked away.
💡 Click the preset pills and templates inside each proposal — the demo is live.

Proposal A: Modern sectioned-card layout Strong recommendation · Bold and solid

Width grows to 720px. Following the conventions of modern Cloud / DevOps panels, the form splits into three logical cards — "task goal and instructions", "schedule and run preview", and "runtime and model environment" — with a clear primary/secondary order and plenty of room for the Prompt.

Proposal B: Two-column engineering workbench (Split Workbench Studio) Heavy-duty developers · Cockpit view

Width grows to 880px. The layout borrows from modern IDEs and pipeline orchestrators (GitHub Actions / Temporal): left workspace (60%) + right inspector (40%). The left side is all about writing the instruction and picking the target; the right side gets a full trigger-pipeline timeline preview.

Proposal C: Stepwise focus wizard (Step Flow Wizard) Low cognitive load · Progressive focus

At 650px wide. The bloated form breaks into three smooth steps: (1) task and schedule, (2) instruction, (3) runtime. Each step shows only its core inputs — clear and unclaustrophobic, which suits the vast majority of ordinary task setups.

✨ Why does the redesign feel more professional and more open?
1
Width goes from 460px to 720px ~ 880px
No more squeezing the toothpaste tube. Controls get room to breathe, and the two-column, card-grouped layout keeps the visual weight balanced.
2
The Prompt becomes a first-class citizen
The thin 4-row box becomes a large monospace-style editor with quick templates (check-up, tests, security, digest), so inspection instructions flow without friction.
3
Plain-language reading and a next-run preview
Pick or type a Cron and the green bar below instantly translates it into plain words and shows the concrete timestamps of the next 2~3 runs — deeply reassuring.
4
Advanced options tucked away elegantly, switches that feel smooth
Model, reasoning level, and downtime catch-up sit in a side-by-side grid with iOS-style Switches instead of jarring native radio buttons.
